A video podcast and the community behind it. Its own description: *leading AI
developers come on to show what they are shipping and the open source tools
behind it.* The format is the point — the hosts **install the software with the
guest and demo it live**, so a claim on the show is something the audience
watched happen rather than something that was described.

It is not a vendor channel. It is a place where a tool is judged by whether it
survives being used on camera by someone who did not build it.

## Who runs it

**Sam Odio** — Facebook, Dropbox, Plow; Sr. Director of New Initiatives at
Dropbox.

**Tom Preston-Werner** — co-founder of GitHub, creator of Jekyll, SemVer and
TOML, and an investor at PWV Ventures. On the show he is deliberately the
student: builders teach him their tools live.

Season one guests include Jesse Vincent (Superpowers), Wes McKinney (Pandas,
Arrow, Ibis), Peter Steinberger (OpenClaw, now at OpenAI), Harper Reed, Naveen
Jain and Logan Kilpatrick.

## What it publishes

Two indexes, and they answer different questions:

| Index | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| **The Agent Index** | which published agents are actually being used |
| **The Builder Index** | which builders are active, and with what tools and models |

An agent's index page carries its install success, active users, active days,
token usage, top users, its activity, and a list of **tasks it completed** with
tags. That last field is the one to read: it is the closest thing to a public
work record, and it is written by the people who ran the agent, not by the
author. See [the Agent Index](/concepts/agent-index.md).

## The community

**Discord — [discord.gg/ZSJZQ7Wmu](https://discord.gg/ZSJZQ7Wmu).** "The room
behind the show." This is where builders get their agent verified and made
one-click deployable, and it is where the hackathon is run. The site's own
`aiworthusing.com/discord` link resolves to the same server under a different
invite code, so both are correct.

**AgentCribs** — the related PWV community for people already building with AI
agents, developer tools and command-line workflows. It began as a private
gathering of PWV founders and is opening selectively; **events are curated,
space is limited, and there is a waitlist application** rather than a door. It
is explicitly not a beginner meetup. The October 6 San Francisco meetup is where
the hackathon winner demos live.

## Why it matters to an agent builder

The community is where the parts a documentation page cannot do happen: getting
**verified**, getting a listing **one-click deployable**, and reaching the
people who are ranking you. The
[publish page](/skills/how-to-publish-your-agent-on-the-agent-index.md) is the
mechanics; the Discord is the escalation.
